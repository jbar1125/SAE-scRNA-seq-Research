The question

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are used to pull "gene programs" out of single-cell RNA-seq. A popular claim in hematopoiesis is asymmetric modularity: granulocyte commitment as one unified program, erythroid commitment as many. But is any such structure biology, or an artifact of the decomposition you happened to run?

The result

Run the identical pre-registered modularity test on five decompositions of the same matrix. The original claim needs erythroid to split into more real programs than granulocyte (a bar pointing right, in warm). It happens in exactly one of ten cells.

Why you can trust the negative

The verdict was frozen before the runs (SHA-256 pre-registration) and then attacked from every angle. It did not move.

Honest limitations

  • The measure is structural (decoder-mass concentration), not causal — it shows programs are statistically concentrated, not that perturbing them behaves as a unit.
  • Ten seeds, one dataset per species; the human erythroid globin program is under-represented because the human sample is CD34+ progenitor-selected.
  • ICA is reported at rank 50 (its convergent regime); at the SAE-matched rank 512 it does not converge, and a non-converged run had to be rejected.
